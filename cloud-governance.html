Case study · Cloud governance

Account lifecycle and resource hygiene

A distributed cloud estate needs consistent handling of ownership, access, lifecycle changes and unused resources. My work combined cloud engineering with the coordination needed to keep those activities moving across teams.

My responsibility
Lifecycle governance, cloud configuration and stakeholder coordination
Scope of work
Account ownership, access policies, resource hygiene and cost reviews
Deliverables
Lifecycle workflows, recurring reviews and operational runbooks

The problem

Operational changes crossed several owners

Account ownership, administrative access and resource usage do not always change together. An account could need a new owner, a review of its access policies or a decision about resources that were no longer required. Handling those activities separately made handoffs and follow-up harder.

My contribution

Connect technical work with accountable owners

I led monthly asset-efficiency reviews and worked on IAM, federation, budgets, alerts and enterprise access policies. As my responsibilities expanded into program management, I coordinated lifecycle and ownership workflows, tracked exceptions and helped document recurring operational tasks.

A delivery decision

Validate ownership and usage before acting

Resource hygiene requires a clear understanding of what a resource supports and who can approve a change. I worked with owners to establish that context, coordinated the required follow-up and used runbooks and automation for recurring work. This kept technical action connected to an accountable decision.

Outcome

A repeatable way to manage the estate

The work produced documented lifecycle and ownership workflows, regular efficiency reviews and reusable operational guidance. Resource hygiene and access governance became part of ongoing operations.
